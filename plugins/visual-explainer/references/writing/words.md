# Words

The reader's own writing style. It applies to every page in every visual style. It also applies to this skill's own files.

## Shape

- Text outside figures is nested bullet lists (`<ul>`). A `<p>` appears only for the lead under the `h1`.
- Each section `h2` is the question the section answers, written without the "?". Its first bullet answers it. Its children elaborate.
- A figcaption is one plain sentence: the claim the figure shows.
- One fact per bullet, under 100 characters.
- A comma separates list items. A clause gets its own sentence or its own child bullet.
- A slogan is a defect. "Show, don't tell" and "Figures lead, words label" become plain sentences.
- A list of two or more things inside a bullet becomes child bullets.
- A bullet that answers, explains or points into the bullet above is its child. Test: delete the line above. If the bullet stops making sense, nest it.
- A parent bullet is a short label of one to four words. What else it wanted to say is its first child.
- A label and its value go on one bullet: `<b>Label:</b> value`, colon inside the bold.
- A stated count matches its children: "Three sources" has three children.
- Lead with an instance. The real command, file, record or output comes before the explanation of it.

## Language

- Literal language. No metaphors, analogies or figurative verbs. Nothing "unlocks", "lives in" or "turns on" anything.
- One term per concept, like a variable name. Never a synonym for something the page already named.
- Short plain sentences: subject, verb, object. The dullest wording that says the fact.
- Nothing is there to sound catchy or melodic. No marketing words, hype, filler or hedges.
- State what is true. Define nothing by what it is not.
- `and` joins alike things. `but` joins contrasting things.
- Expand an acronym on the line of its first use.
- Every factual claim carries an inline source link at the point of the claim. Flag an unconfirmed claim as unconfirmed.
- No trailing summaries. No meta-comments about the page.

## Numbers and characters

- A number carries its unit in every label and states its comparison (`from 5,600`).
- Numbers are formatted for reading: `5.6k`, `−86%`.
- A no-break space sits between a number and its unit: `1&nbsp;ms`.
- Characters: curly quotes, `–` for ranges, `−` for minus, `×` for multipliers, `…` and `≤`.
- No em dash. A break between two thoughts is a period.
- Emphasis is bold or italic. One phrase never gets both.
- Italics and uppercase only for 3 words or fewer.
- Underline only links and linked terms.
